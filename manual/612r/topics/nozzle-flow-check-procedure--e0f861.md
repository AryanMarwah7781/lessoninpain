Procedure to Perform:
IMPORTANT: Do not perform nozzle flow check with chemicals in tank. Incorrect application may result in crop damage or environment contamination.

6. Note the actual application rate and solution flow rate displayed on the screen. Actual application rate is shown in either liters per hectare (L/ha) or gallons per acre (gal/ac). Solution flow rate is shown in either liters per minute (L/min) or gallons per minute (gal/min).
7. Hold a known volume container, such as a calibration container, under a nozzle and collect the output for 1 minute. Record the volume of output.
8. Collect and record the output of three to four additional nozzles for 1 minute per nozzle.

N118396—UN—17FEB16

![[N118396.png]]
**Figure N118396**

Close
10. Select the Close button to return to the previous page.
11. Calculate the average of the nozzle output samples that were collected. To calculate the average nozzle output, add all the nozzle output samples. Divide the sum by the number of nozzle output samples.
Sample 1 + Sample 2 + Sample 3 + Sample 4 = Sum
Sum ÷ 4 = Average Nozzle Output
12. Multiply the average nozzle output by the total number of nozzles on the boom.
13. Convert this value from milliliters or ounces to liters or gallons. The converted value is the calculated flow rate of the entire boom in liters per minute or gallons per minute.
- To convert from milliliters to liters, move the decimal point of the milliliter value 3 places to the left.
Metric Unit of Measurement Example:
85,171.77 mL = 85.17177 L = 85.17 L/min flow rate
- To convert from ounces to gallons, divide the value in ounces by 128.
Standard Unit of Measurement Example:
2880 ounces ÷ 128 = 22.5 gallons = 22.5 gal/min flow rate
14. Compare the calculated flow rate to the solution flow rate that was displayed on the screen. Calculate the difference between the two values as a percentage. To calculate the difference: Calculated Flow Rate - Solution Flow Rate = Difference
Difference ÷ Solution Flow Rate x 100 = Difference in Percent
Metric Unit of Measurement Example:
85.17 L/min - 83.28 L/min = 1.89
1.89 ÷ 83.28 x 100 = 2.27%
Standard Unit of Measurement Example:
22.5 gal/min - 22 gal/min = 0.5
0.5 ÷ 22 x 100 = 2.27%
- If the calculated flow rate and solution flow rate differ by more than 5%, check the flow meter, the Flow Meter calibration, and strainers.
- If the pressure is lower than expected, check the orifice valves, the pressure sensor, the Pressure Sensor calibration, and the nozzle tips for wear.
- If the pressure is higher than expected for the given output, check the pressure sensor, the Pressure Sensor calibration, the strainers, for a partially plugged nozzle tip or nozzle body, and for plugs elsewhere in the spray system.
- High flow rates can cause a pressure drop in the boom lines which results in lower pressure in the boom.
15. Calculate the application rate and compare it to the actual application rate that was displayed on the screen. To calculate the application rate: Constant Value ÷ Boom Width x Calculated Flow Rate ÷ Machine Speed = Rate per Unit of Area
NOTE: The constant value for metric unit of measurement calculations is 600. The constant value for standard unit of measurement calculations is 495.
Metric Unit of Measurement Example:
600 ÷ 27.43 m x 85.17 L/min ÷ 16.1 km/h = 115.71 L/ha
Standard Unit of Measurement Example:
495 ÷ 90 ft x 22.5 gal/min ÷ 10 mph = 12.375 gal/ac
If the calculated application rate and the actual application rate that was displayed on the screen differ by more than 5%:
- Check the Flow Meter calibration.
- Check the strainers.
- Check the minimum pressure setting in the Spray System application.
- Check the Pressure Sensor calibration.
- Check the air bleed line.
- Verify that the bypass valve is not stuck open.
- Check for leaks at the pump.
- Check for blockages at the pump inlet.
