# Set Up Nurse Vehicle Plumbing

NOTE: Picture illustrates nurse tank setup example with maximum recommended requirements for optimal performance.
N87548—UN—11JAN10

![[N87548.png]]
**Figure N87548** — A: Nurse Tank; B: Nurse Tank Vent; C: Nurse Tank Outlet; D: Hose; E: Shutoff Valve; F: Hose; G: Nurse Arm Coupler

Nurse vehicle plumbing should not include more than one 102 mm (4 in) full port shutoff (ball) valve, no more than 9.14 m (30 ft) of hose (D and F) between the nurse arm coupler (G) and nurse tank outlet (C), and vertical distance between the nurse tank fluid level and sprayer coupler should not exceed 914 mm (3 ft). Additional fittings reduce performance and increase fill times.
If customer installs additional plumbing and accepts reduced performance, see GUIDELINES FOR NURSE VEHICLE PLUMBING in following section.
Requirements for tank venting:
Inadequate venting can cause nurse tank collapse or slow fill time.
- Use 51 mm (2 in) diameter or larger vent (B). 76 mm (3 in) vent may result in better performance.
- If using a restrictive vent (example: labyrinth or check valve style), consider multiple or larger-size vents.
Requirements for the nurse arm hose:
Customer is responsible for purchasing nurse arm hose and clamps. Nurse arm and coupler are designed for 102 mm (4 in) hose. It is strongly recommended that John Deere-approved hose (PM180HR400) and hose clamps (AN306648) be used as they meet system requirements. Any alternative hose must meet following requirements:
- Minimum bend radius: 152 mm (6 in)
- Minimum vacuum rating: 29 in/Hg
- Minimum pressure rating: 41.37 kPa (0.41 bar) (30 psi)
- Smooth wall inner bore
- Compatible with agriculture chemicals and fertilizer
IMPORTANT: Debris can cause system damage, leaks, or reduced performance. Filtering sprayer solution with size 16 mesh strainer or finer is recommended. Filter before or while filling nurse tank. Do not filter between the nurse tank and nurse arm coupler. Nurse arm filtration results in debris buildup and reduced performance.
Shutoff valve is recommended in the flow path between the nurse tank outlet and nurse arm coupler. Shutoff valve MUST be closed when filling nurse tank.
Do NOT pressurize system beyond the rating of hose and components or damage could occur.
Guidelines for Nurse Vehicle Plumbing:
IMPORTANT: Load Command system is sensitive to flow path restrictions between the nurse tank and nurse arm coupler. Restrictions could result in slower fill times and reduced performance. Use the following recommendations when plumbing coupler to tank.

N84437—UN—23APR09

![[N84437.png]]
**Figure N84437** — A: Straight Flow Path; B: Turn in Flow Path; C: Turn in Flow Path; D: Elbow Fitting; E: T-Fitting; F: Cross Fitting

- Keep straight flow (A) through T-fittings and cross fittings. Avoid turns (B and C) in the flow path.
- Swept fittings are preferred over fittings with sharp internal corners. Minimize number of elbow fittings (D), T-fittings (E), and cross fittings (F) used.
- Do not use strainers and check valves.
- Do not use 3-way, angle, or globe shutoff valves or any valves that do not have a straight flow path through them.

N137656—UN—21MAY18

- 2-Way ball valves (A) are recommended as long as they maintain full diameter (B) of plumbing, such as 102 mm (4 in).
- Butterfly valves (C) are more restrictive than ball valves and should be larger in size than feed line.
N137657—UN—21MAY18

![[N137657.png]]
**Figure N137657** — A: Inside of Hose; B: Transition Point; C: Inside of Fitting

- Hose inside (A) should be smooth. Do not use hoses with ribs on inside.
- Minimize number of transition points (B), such as hose barbs, union, nipple, or reducer fittings. Avoid fittings (C) with large wall thickness at transition points. Metal fittings will usually be thinner than plastic.
- Operating multiple supply lines in parallel at the same time can reduce restrictions.
Verify that plumbing joints do not leak. Air leaks during the filling result in reduced performance. Coat threads of plumbing joints with PM37397 Pipe Sealant with TEFLON or equivalent to prevent leaks.
If multiple tanks are plumbed together, they must have minimum of 102 mm (4 in) plumbing and must be open to nurse arm at same time during fill to reduce restrictions. Using 102 mm (4 in) or larger plumbing reduces restrictions and improves performance.
