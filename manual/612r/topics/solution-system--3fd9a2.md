This sprayer is equipped with a stainless steel dual product solution tank that is divided into two separate reservoirs. A smaller reservoir (tank 1) is intended for spot spray application. A larger reservoir (tank 2) is intended for broadcast spray application. Solution tanks are available with the following fluid capacities:

Each solution tank is equipped with a fill neck and lid assembly. The solution tanks can be filled through the fill necks or through the main Quik-Fill valve at the side load station on the left-hand side of the machine or the front load station on the front right-hand side of the machine. Solution can be loaded into the tanks from a nurse tank connected to the Quik-Fill valve at either load station. Solution can be pushed on using a pump provided with the nurse tank or can be pulled on using the onboard POWRSPRAY pump. A suction strainer is located in the front fill line dedicated to filling tank 1. The strainer is constructed of a noncorrosive material that must be cleaned as required. On the left-hand side of the machine, there are clear tubes and graduated scales that indicate the solution levels in liters and gallons for each tank system.

- Drain fluid from the respective solution tank.

The solution pumps (12 and 47) are a centrifugal type that are driven by hydraulic motors. Engagement and disengagement of the system 2 pump is controlled by a button on the CommandARM armrest. The system 1 pump is controlled using soft keys on the CommandCenter display. To help prevent pump cavitation, any air trapped in the pumps is allowed to escape through the solution pump bleed line (8 and 51).
Strainers (18 and 41) are located in the pressure supply lines before the solution flowmeters (19 and 40). The strainers remove sediment before it reaches the flowmeters and boom valves.
Immediately following the flowmeters are shutoff valves (20 and 39), which can be closed to allow servicing of the strainers (18 and 41) and flowmeters (19 and 40).
A high flow rate product application can be achieved by using the single combined tank mode. The single combined tank mode's high flow rate can be selected from the CommandCenter display in the operator’s station cab.
Agitation nozzles are mounted in four locations in the bottom of each tank. The nozzles create a vigorous fan-type agitation throughout the tank to keep chemicals, such as wettable powders, in suspension. The agitation valves (9 and 44) control the agitation flow and have three settings. These three settings can be selected by the operator using the CommandCenter display in the operator’s station cab. The agitation valves can be opened using a button on the side load station keypad or by pressing a button on the CommandARM armrest in the operator’s station cab.
The tank rinse / eductor valves (14 and 45) have two functions. These include:
- Directing solution fluid to the tank rinse nozzles at the top of the solution tanks.
- Directing solution fluid to the eductor venturi.
The eductor venturi creates a vacuum to pull the chemical in the eductor hoppers (17 and 42) into the solution stream. The eductor hopper rinse valves (15 and 43) direct solution to the hopper rinse nozzles. The eductor hopper suction valves allow chemical to be drawn from the bottom of the eductor hopper to the solution tanks (7 and 52).
Rinse nozzles in the solution tank are used during a rinse cycle to clean the inside walls of the tank.
Check valves are used in the system to prevent solution from flowing in the wrong direction.
Spray section valves are electrically actuated valves that are opened or closed using soft keys on the CommandCenter display or IBS buttons on the multi-function lever.
The spray nozzles located on the center frame and booms are used to meter, atomize, and dispense the solution into specific patterns. Solution flow is metered by the size of the orifice in the nozzle tip. Within limits, solution flow through a nozzle can be increased or decreased by adjusting system pressure.
Most nozzles are designed for optimum performance at specific pressures. However, the range of adjustment is relatively narrow. Line pressures that are too high or too low affect the atomization of the solution and create variations in the spray pattern. The solution atomizes when the liquid is forced through the orifice in the nozzle. The shape of the spray pattern is determined by the shape of the orifice.
The rinse tank (62) can be loaded with clean water through the Quik-Fill valves at the side fill station or through the main Quik-Fill valve at the front fill station. Water from the rinse tank is used when rinsing the solution system or the eductors.
System Operation
When the solution pumps (12 and 47) is activated, solution is drawn from the corresponding solution tanks (7 or 52) through the solution tank sump valves (10 and 49). The pump pushes the solution through the pressure strainers (18 and 41) and flowmeters (19 and 40) to the spray control valves, and to the agitation circuit through the agitation valves (9 and 44).
When the spray control valves are open, the solution flows to the nozzles, where it is atomized and sprayed.
The flowmeters (19 and 40) send the flow rate information to a control unit, which compares that signal to the operator-programmed application rate. The flowmeter and boom pressure sensor calculate the flow information and send to a control unit. If there is a difference in the signal from the flowmeters and the programmed rate, the proportional valve assembly receives more or less hydraulic flow until the system is stabilized and within the tolerance band of the targeted application rate.
When the compensator opens, more hydraulic oil is sent to the hydraulic motor, causing the solution pump to increase its flow. When the compensator closes, less hydraulic oil is sent to the hydraulic motor, causing the solution pump to decrease its flow. While operating in Auto mode, the system automatically raises the engine speed to provide enough hydraulic pump flow to the boom for the given target flow rate.
The control unit provides the ability to maintain application, within a tolerance range of application rates, while changing speeds. Application rate consistency depends on a number of things, such as nozzle tip size, target application rate, and rate of speed change. The control unit provides quick response, but some differences in application rates can be observed if the operator makes rapid speed changes or operates at the extremes of the solution system’s capacity.
Key inputs to the control unit, used for rate control, include the GPS speed, wheel speed sensors, solution pump speed sensor, solution pump outlet pressure sensor, flowmeters, and boom pressure sensor. The control unit has an internal warning system that alerts the operator if the actual flow outside of the tolerance band varies with the target application rate. The system is a volume-regulating system, so pressures displayed on the secondary user interface display may vary at a given ground speed.
The solution pressure required for a given application rate is determined by speed and nozzle tip size. Before beginning an application, refer to a nozzle tip calculator or nozzle tip selection chart, and make sure that the spray boom is equipped with spray tip nozzles that provide desired application rates at desired pressures and speeds. Then, make sure to go through the spray setup on the display and input the nozzle tips being used. The display provides feedback on the tip selected for the application rate targeted.
The spray system master switch on the multi-function lever and on the filling station keypad are integral parts of the system. When OFF is selected, electrical power closes the boom valves and the variable solution pump slows to maintain spray-off pressure (as set by the operator in the spray setup).
Solution may continue to spray from the boom for a short time after the spray system master switch OFF is selected. This condition is due to pressure remaining in the line. Purging air out of boom lines greatly improves response times. The time interval that spray continues flowing depends upon pressure in the spray line, boom hose lengths and routings, volume of air in the boom, nozzle tip size, and use of the visual nozzle check valves. In order to compensate for the boom shutoff lag time, the spray system master switch OFF should be selected before reaching the end row.
When spray system master switch ON is selected, the variable solution pump maintains the minimum solution pressure, as set by the operator, if the machine is moving slowly or is not moving. This action may result in an application rate that is higher than the target application rate. Likewise, if the machine is moving too fast, the variable solution pump maintains the maximum solution pressure. This action may result in an application rate that is lower than the target application rate.
