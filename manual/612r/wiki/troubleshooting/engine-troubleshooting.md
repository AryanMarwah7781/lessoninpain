## Warnings for the fixes

> [!warning] CAUTION — printed on [[relieve-fuel-system-pressure--3d6e3c|Relieve Fuel System Pressure]] (p. 95-6). Applies to: fuel lines — bleeding and flushing the fuel system
> CAUTION: Escaping diesel fuel under pressure can have sufficient force to penetrate the skin, causing serious injury. Before disconnecting lines, be sure to relieve pressure. Before applying pressure to the system, be sure ALL connections are tight and lines, pipes and hoses are not damaged. Keep hands and body away from pinholes and nozzles which eject fluid under pressure. Use a piece of cardboard or wood, rather than hands, to search for suspected leaks. If ANY fluid is injected into the skin, it must be surgically removed within a few hours by a doctor familiar with this type injury or gangrene may result. Doctors unfamiliar with this type of injury may call the Deere & Company Medical Department in Moline, Illinois, or other knowledgeable medical source.

> [!warning] CAUTION — printed on [[relieve-fuel-system-pressure--3d6e3c|Relieve Fuel System Pressure]] (p. 95-6). Applies to: HPCR fuel system
> CAUTION: High-pressure fluid remaining in fuel lines can cause serious injury. Only technicians familiar with this type of system should perform repairs. Before disconnecting fuel lines, sensors, or any other components between the high-pressure fuel pump and nozzles on engines with High Pressure Common Rail (HPCR) fuel system, wait a minimum of 15 minutes after engine is stopped.

> [!warning] CAUTION — printed on [[replace-fuel-filters-500-hours-6-8-l-engine--01999c|Replace Fuel Filters—500 Hours (6.8 L Engine)]] (p. 95-8, 95-9); [[replace-fuel-filters-500-hours-6-8-l-engine--2b94e9|Replace Fuel Filters—500 Hours (6.8 L Engine)]] (p. 115-32, 115-33). Applies to: replacing fuel filters
> CAUTION: Fuel in filters can be under pressure. Open valve (A) on bottom of the water separator bowl to relieve pressure before removing the filter.

> [!warning] CAUTION — printed on [[replace-fuel-filters-500-hours-9-0-l-engine--3400ab|Replace Fuel Filters—500 Hours (9.0 L Engine)]] (p. 95-9, 95-10); [[replace-fuel-filters-500-hours-9-0-l-engine--e41111|Replace Fuel Filters—500 Hours (9.0 L Engine)]] (p. 115-33). Applies to: replacing fuel filters
> CAUTION: Fuel in filters can be under pressure. Open valve (A) on bottom of the water separator bowl to relieve pressure before removing filter.

> [!warning] CAUTION — printed on [[service-cooling-system-safely--04c2de|Service Cooling System Safely]] (p. 05-8). Applies to: coolant level, radiator, thermostat
> CAUTION: Explosive release of fluids from pressurized cooling system can cause serious burns. Shut off engine, set park brake, and remove key. NEVER add coolant when engine is overheated. Wait for it to cool. NEVER remove surge tank cap when coolant or engine is hot. Wait until engine coolant is cool to remove cap. SLOWLY loosen cap to relieve pressure before removing completely.

> [!warning] CAUTION — printed on [[servicing-batteries--e9a1c5|Servicing Batteries]] (p. 85-3). Applies to: battery checks
> CAUTION: Batteries can explode causing serious injury or death to you or others. Do not attempt to service batteries unless you have the proper equipment and experience to perform the job. Have it done by a John Deere dealer or other service provider.

> [!warning] CAUTION — printed on [[service-air-intake-system-1000-hours-or-annually--590bfa|Service Air Intake System—1000 Hours or Annually]] (p. 115-46, 115-47). Applies to: servicing air filters
> CAUTION: Avoid serious injury from falling. Do not stand on the fuel tank when replacing air filters.

More fuel and battery warnings: [[fuel-fire-and-battery-safety]].

## Engine hard to start, will not start.

| Problem | Solution |
|---|---|
| Fuel tank empty. | Add fuel and bleed fuel system. |
| Water, dirt, or air in the fuel system. | Drain, flush, fill, and bleed fuel system. |
| Improper type or old fuel. | Consult fuel chart and use the proper type of fuel for operating conditions. |
| Plugged fuel filters. | Replace filter elements and bleed system. |
| Plugged fuel tank strainer. | Drain fuel tank, remove, and clean tank strainer. |
| Low battery output. | Check battery voltage. Clean battery. Charge if necessary. |
| Excessive resistance in the starting circuit. | Clean and tighten all connections on battery and starter. |
| Safety switch not activated. | Move multi-function lever to neutral. Inspect switch. |
| Solenoid disconnected. | Check wiring. |
| Fuel pump primer lever position. | Place lever in down position. |
| Faulty injection pump solenoid. | Replace solenoid. |
| Crankcase oil of too heavy viscosity. | Drain and fill crankcase with John Deere Plus-50 II oil of proper viscosity and quality. |
| Low compression. | Contact a John Deere dealer or other service provider. |
| Sticking valves. | Contact a John Deere dealer or other service provider. |
| Dirty or faulty injectors. | Contact a John Deere dealer or other service provider. |
| Cold weather. | Use cold weather starting aids. |

| Problem | Solution |
|---|---|
| Low fuel level. | Add fuel and check system. |
| Clogged fuel filters. | Replace filter element and bleed system. |
| Plugged fuel tank strainer. | Drain fuel tank, remove, and clean tank strainer. |
| Water, dirt, or air in the fuel system. | Drain, flush, fill, and bleed system. Tighten fuel connections. |

| Solenoid wire loose. | Check wiring. |
| Dirty or faulty injectors. | Contact a John Deere dealer or other service provider. |
| Injection pump out of time. | Contact a John Deere dealer or other service provider. |
| Injection pump speed advance. | Contact a John Deere dealer or other service provider. |
| Bent push rods or sticky valves. | Contact a John Deere dealer or other service provider. |
| Fuel tank vent obstructed. | Clean vent in solvent, then blow dry. |

## Engine overheats.

| Problem | Solution |
|---|---|
| Engine overloaded. | Reduce load. |
| Loose or defective fan belt. | Replace belt. |
| Low coolant level. | Fill radiator with coolant to the proper level. Check hoses and radiator for leaks or loose connections. |
| Dirty cooling system radiator core, air conditioner condenser, or engine side screens. | Remove all foreign matter from exterior or radiator core, air conditioner condenser, and screens. Steam clean if necessary. |
| Cooling system internally restricted. | Drain and flush cooling system. |
| Defective thermostat, pressure cap, switch, or gauge. | Remove and check. |
| Low engine oil level. | Check oil level. Add as necessary. |
| Wrong oil viscosity. | Change to proper oil viscosity. |
| Engine timed too late. | Contact a John Deere dealer or other service provider. |
| Defective thermostats. | Replace thermostats. |
| Leaking head gasket. | Contact a John Deere dealer or other service provider. |

## Engine emits black or gray exhaust smoke.

| Problem | Solution |
|---|---|
| Improper fuel type. | Use the proper type of fuel for operating conditions. |
| Engine overloaded. | Reduce load. |
| Clogged or dirty air cleaner. | Check air cleaner for restrictions. Be sure that filter element is clean. |
| Dirty or faulty injectors. | Contact a John Deere dealer or other service provider. |
| Air in the fuel system. | Bleed fuel system. Check all connections and fuel tank level. |
| Defective muffler. | Check the muffler for possible damage that might create backpressure. Clean muffler. |
| Engine out of time. | Make sure that injection pump is timed properly. Contact a John Deere dealer or other service provider. |

## Engine emits white exhaust smoke.

| Problem | Solution |
|---|---|
| Cold engine. | Warm engine to the normal operating temperatures. |
| Clogged or dirty air cleaner. | Service air cleaner. |
| Improper type of fuel. | Low octane fuel causes misfiring. Only use fuel that has recommended cetane or octane number. |
| Injection nozzles dirty. | Contact a John Deere dealer or other service provider. |
| Thermostat defective or too cold. | Remove and check thermostat. |
| Turbocharger not functioning. | Contact a John Deere dealer or other service provider. |
| Engine out of time. | Make sure that injection pump is timed properly. Contact a John Deere dealer or other service provider. |
